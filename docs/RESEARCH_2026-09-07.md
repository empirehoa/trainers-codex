## Summary

The verified evidence base is narrow: of the five research areas, only sharing (area 2), daily/archive retention design (area 3), and fan-project monetization/legal posture (area 5) produced claims that survived adversarial verification; nothing on decision-vs-RNG balance (area 1, beyond the WordleBot precedent) or mobile-first browser UX (area 4) survived, so those decisions cannot be grounded in this report. What did survive points in a consistent direction: Wordle's spoiler-free emoji grid was a player invention that Wardle productized, growth went from under 1,000 to ~2M players in weeks, and yet only ~5% of players on a peak day posted grids publicly on Twitter, so the share artifact should be optimized for low-friction, off-platform sharing rather than public-feed volume. NYT then treated the 1,000+ puzzle archive (with saved, shareable results) and WordleBot's post-game skill-vs-luck analysis as paid subscriber features while keeping the daily free, which is the clearest precedent for Journey Mode's archive and 'what-if' surfaces as premium levers. On the fan-tool side, PokéRogue openly names Pokémon, runs a real Go backend, and takes donations strictly for infrastructure with no cosmetics or tiers, and even so converts only a handful of sponsors (5 current / 89 past on GitHub Sponsors against 1M+ accounts); TPC's former CLO testified that crowdfunding/monetization, together with press, was what triggered enforcement through 2020, though Nintendo took down free fan projects in the same period. Net implication: the share and archive patterns are well supported and buildable now; the merch/premium upsell sits directly on the line the rights holder and the community have historically policed, and the balance question remains open.

## Caveats the harness raised about its own coverage

Coverage is badly uneven relative to the five-part question. Nothing survived verification for area 1 (how Balatro, Slay the Spire 2, Luck be a Landlord, Vampire Survivors etc. make decisions beat RNG: interest/compounding economies, synergy drafting, rerolls, risk mitigation as skill); every Balatro/auto-chess claim from gangles.ca was refuted 0-3 because the source was egress-blocked and the text could not be read. Nothing at all survived for area 4 (mobile-first browser UX, time-to-first-choice, one-thumb interaction, iOS Safari pitfalls), and no Connections, Cine2Nerdle, Pikalytics, Showdown, Infinite Fusion, or Marriland evidence survived. So the report cannot ground the balance fix that the audit demands; the WordleBot skill-vs-luck precedent is the only design-relevant survivor there. No source anywhere in the surviving set contains measured share rates, DAU, D7/D30 retention, or A/B results; the Wordle ~5% public-share figure is a one-day scraper lower bound and every retention-design claim (archive gating, WordleBot) is a product announcement, not an outcome measurement. Wardle's growth attributions are self-reported and anecdotal. Source access was severely degraded: techcrunch.com, aftermath.site, nytimes.com, gangles.ca, Wikipedia and archive.org were all blocked by the egress proxy and the WebSearch budget was exhausted, so several quotes are search-index excerpts corroborated through secondary outlets rather than fetched page text (flagged per finding). Time sensitivity: McGowan's testimony describes TPC practice only through 2020 and enforcement posture may have changed (Relic Castle 2024 shows TPCi acting against a non-commercial site); PokéRogue sponsor counts and the absence of a takedown are as of 2026-09-07 and can change; the NYT archive/WordleBot paywall status reflects 2024-2026 reporting. The PokéRogue donation-conversion inference is n=1 through a high-friction channel and should not be generalized to paid merch conversion, for which no data was found. Legal findings are background for a product decision, not legal advice; the rights-holder question should go to IP counsel before any press launch paired with a paid upsell.

## What survived verification (8 claims)

### 1. Wordle's viral share artifact (the spoiler-free colored-square grid) was invented by players, not the designer: a New Zealand early adopter hand-typed emoji squares on Twitter, and Wardle then built the share button to reproduce that format (added mid-December 2021).

**Confidence:** medium · **Vote:** 3-0 (claims 3 and 5 merged)

Wordle's viral share artifact (the spoiler-free colored-square grid) was invented by players, not the designer: a New Zealand early adopter hand-typed emoji squares on Twitter, and Wardle then built the share button to reproduce that format (added mid-December 2021). Over that period the game grew from fewer than 1,000 to ~2 million players (Nov 2021 to ~Jan 10-12, 2022). However, most players never posted publicly: Wardle estimated the Wordle Stats scraper captured ~100,000 grids on a day when close to 2 million people played (~5%), and the primary bot tweet for Wordle #205 confirms 107,134 results found on Twitter. Design implication for Journey Mode: the Legend Card/clip should be optimized for one-tap sharing into private channels and for being legible without context, since public-feed volume was a minority of actual sharing; the remaining ~95% either shared privately or not at all, and the sources do not say which.

**Evidence.** TechCrunch interview (Wardle): 'the majority of players are not actually posting their results on Twitter'; 'over the space of weeks, the game grew from fewer than 1,000 to 2 million players'. Corroborated by the @WordleStats primary tweet (107,134 results, 2022-01-10) and by NYT (90 players Nov 1 2021; 300k Jan 2 2022) and Guardian/BBC (~2M ~Jan 10-12). No measured share rate or A/B data exists anywhere in these sources; the causal link between the grid and growth is Wardle's and journalists' inference. The 5% figure is a one-day lower bound from a scraper that admitted undercounting; Twitter's engineering blog reports ~500K Wordle tweets/day at peak, so the public fraction was higher later in January. techcrunch.com was egress-blocked; quotes recovered from search-index snippets and syndicated copies.

**Sources.** <https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/12/josh-wardle-interview-wordle/> · <https://x.com/WordleStats/status/1480947644130369545>

## What this means for Journey Mode

1. **Skill-vs-luck readout — build now.** WordleBot is the only verified precedent for the audit's core problem: a player cannot tell whether they played well or rolled well. The engine is deterministic, so the honest version is cheap — replay the finished seed under fixed strategies and show where the player's choices landed in this seed's range, and how this seed's dice compared to an average career. NYT ships this as a *subscriber* feature; it ships free here.
2. **The text share artifact already exists — make it visible.** `buildShareText` produces the rank line + emoji badge strip. It is copied by the button labelled "Copy link", which hides the artifact the research says carries the game. Show it on screen and label the button for what it does.
3. **Archive as a premium lever — product decision, not code.** NYT gated 1,000+ past puzzles behind the subscription while keeping the daily free; archive play does not count toward streaks. Journey's `?issue=N` archive is free today. Whether to gate it is Jose's call; the precedent is recorded here.
4. **Monetization posture — confirms the existing gate.** PokéRogue takes cost-recovery donations only, names Pokémon openly, and has drawn no documented takedown; TPC's former CLO named *taking money* plus *press* as the trigger, while Nintendo took down free projects in the same period. `JOURNEY_MERCH_CTA` stays off until counsel clears it, and a Product Hunt / HN launch is itself the exposure event.
5. **Balance — no external grounding; stands on internal measurement.** Nothing on decision-vs-RNG design survived verification. The v14 risk-payoff rebalance rests on the repo's own 3,000-career sweeps (`journey/risk.test.ts`), not on this report.

```
Research question for a product decision. Context: "Trainer's Codex" is a single-file web Pokémon team builder + coverage analyzer that also ships "Journey Mode" — a deterministic, seed-shareable, 3-minute choose-your-path trainer-career roguelite ending in a shareable 1080×1350 Legend Card and a 9:16 video clip, with a daily seed, streaks, an archive of past dailies, named rank tiers with percentiles, and an optional print-on-demand merch upsell. An internal audit just measured that in Journey Mode the safe choice strictly dominates for every archetype and seed-to-seed variance (SD 83–108 points) outweighs every player decision in a run combined (spread 34–90) — the dice matter more than the player.

Question: What do the most successful 2024–2026 daily/share-driven roguelites and puzzle games (Balatro, Wordle and its clones, Slay the Spire 2 dailies, Luck be a Landlord, Vampire Survivors, Connections, Cine2Nerdle, Pokémon-adjacent fan tools like Pikalytics, Pokémon Showdown, PokéRogue, Pokémon Infinite Fusion, Marriland) do — concretely and verifiably — in these five areas, and what does the evidence say actually moves retention and sharing:

3. Daily/streak/archive retention design: streak forgiveness, catch-up mechanics, archive access, leaderboards/percentiles, and what the data says about DAU and D7/D30 retention effects.

Prioritize primary sources: developer postmortems, GDC/talks, Steam/App Store data, official blog posts, court filings/DMCA records, and quantified case studies over opinion pieces.
```
